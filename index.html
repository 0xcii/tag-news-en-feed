Justice Kant added that bitcoin (BTC) trading is "an illicit trade more or less like a Hawala business," referring to a term used for the informal transferring of money from one place to another without the actual movement of physical money. They were speaking while hearing a bail petition filed by Shailesh Babulal Bhatt, a Gujarat resident accused of crypto-related fraud. Bhati had claimed Bhatt was one of the biggest BTC trade aggregators in Gujarat, victimizing others with promises of high returns. The court however said that it could not ascertain whether Bhatt was a victimizer or a victim, lamenting the government's inability to come up with a clear regime regulating cryptocurrency. India's government had plans to release a discussion paper outlining its crypto policy stance by September last year, though this has yet to materialize.
